Fifteen days in Kyrgyzstan, from Bishkek to the high plateaus of Son-Kul and Köl-Suu, then around Issyk-Kul before heading back through the Chuy valley. A little over 2,100 km by car, a good share of it on dirt tracks. Each region has its map, its stops and the practical notes worth knowing before you go.

<Before>

The country does not suit a tight schedule. Distances look short on the map, but a mountain track is driven at 30 km/h on a good day, and the weather turns quickly above 3,000 m. We planned with room to spare, and that saved several days: a pass closed by snow one morning, a track rougher than described, a herd holding the road for twenty minutes.

As for the car, a high-clearance 4×4 is not a luxury: on Kalmak-Ashuu, the Köl-Suu track and the climb to Altyn Arashan, a saloon will not get through. Bishkek rental agencies usually supply two spare wheels; check them before you leave, and the jack too.

<Tip kind="border">
Köl-Suu and the area near the Chinese border need a border permit. You pick it up in Naryn, but check the lead time and the documents required before you leave: without it, the track ends at the first checkpoint.
</Tip>

<Tip kind="money">
Outside Bishkek and Karakol, almost everything is paid in cash: yurt camps, fuel, meals. Leave with enough som for several days.
</Tip>

<Tip kind="info">
A local SIM card bought in Bishkek covers the towns and main roads. On the plateaus and tracks, rely on an offline map: download this page's GPX before you go.
</Tip>

</Before>

<Region id="bichkek">

<Day n={1} title="Arrival in Bishkek" />

Morning arrival at Manas after a connection in Istanbul. The day goes to the city centre: Ala-Too Square, the Soviet architecture walk and the main museums.

<Day n={2} title="Bishkek on foot" />

Bishkek is easy to see on foot. The avenues are wide and tree-lined, and the centre fits in a rectangle about twenty minutes' walk on each side. It is also the place to sort out logistics before leaving: a local SIM card, cash, and one last shop for the days without stores.

In the evening, Osh bazaar is worth the trip for its sheer size more than for shopping. It has everything for the coming days' picnics: dried fruit, flatbread, sheep's cheese and a thermos of tea.

<Tip kind="money">
The transfer from Manas airport costs about 1,800 KGS for two in a taxi booked through the hotel. Have som in cash from the moment you land.
</Tip>

<Regrets>

- Sort out the SIM card and cash on the first day, not on the morning we left.

</Regrets>

</Region>

<Region id="son-koul">

<Day n={3} title="Bishkek → Kochkor → Son-Kul" />

Pick up the car in Bishkek and head for Kochkor for the regional museum and the bazaar. From there, the climb to Son-Kul goes over the Kalmak-Ashuu pass.

The first 200 km to Kochkor are paved and fast. The landscape changes all at once after Balykchy: orchards give way to short steppe, then to the first golden slopes. Kochkor is the last real town before the lake, with a fuel station and grocery shops.

<Photo id="DSC01117" />

<Tip kind="road" at="kalmak-ashuu">
The Kalmak-Ashuu road is a mountain track, described as off-road for much of its 120 km. Allow 2 h 30 from Kochkor and don't set off after mid-afternoon.
</Tip>

The pass itself is spectacular: a string of tight hairpins on a bare slope, then the plateau opens up all at once, vast, above 3,000 m. The lake shows in the distance, bluer than the sky.

<Day n={4} title="On the shore of Son-Kul" />

A full day at the lake: walks around the camp, an optional horse ride, and the herding life that sets the pace on the plateau.

<Photo id="DSC01163" />

A day at Son-Kul runs on the herds' clock. In the morning the horses are led to the water; in the afternoon the sheep come down the slopes; in the evening everyone is back before the wind rises. There is not much to do, and that is exactly the point.

<Tip kind="sleep" at="son-koul-camp">
The yurt camps on the south shore have no signal and no constant power. Nights drop below freezing in late September: bring a proper sleeping bag.
</Tip>

The night is the real show. With no light for dozens of kilometres, the Milky Way reads with the naked eye and the stars come down to the horizon. Bring a tripod if you shoot: the wind only drops after midnight.

<Regrets>

- Leave Kochkor earlier for Son-Kul: the [Kalmak-Ashuu track](#pt-kalmak-ashuu) is longer than it looks.

</Regrets>

</Region>

<Region id="naryn">

<Day n={5} title="Son-Kul → Naryn → Köl-Suu" />

Down from Son-Kul towards Naryn over the Moldo-Ashuu pass and Ak Tal, still on dirt.

The descent over Moldo-Ashuu is shorter than the climb over Kalmak-Ashuu, but steeper: some thirty hairpins, taken in first gear. Below, the Naryn valley is wide and dry, dotted with villages and cemeteries of earthen mausoleums.

Naryn is the region's stopover town: one long main street along the river, simple hotels, and above all the office that issues the border permits for Köl-Suu.

<Tip kind="border" at="naryn-permit">
The Köl-Suu area is a border zone: pick up the permit in Naryn before going on. The office is in a building with a red lattice front. Without a permit, the checkpoints turn you back.
</Tip>

<Tip kind="road" at="kol-suu-track">
After Naryn, about 130 km and 4 h of mountain track to the Kychtto jailoo. Fill up in Naryn: there is nothing after.
</Tip>

<Tip kind="fuel">
Beyond Naryn and At-Bashy, fuel stations are few and not always supplied. A 20-litre jerrycan saves counting every kilometre.
</Tip>

The track to Kychtto passes two military checkpoints, then climbs an ever narrower valley. The last kilometres follow the river up to the jailoo, a summer pasture where a few families pitch their yurts.

<Photo id="DSC01520" />

<Day n={6} title="Köl-Suu lake, then Tash Rabat" />

The next day, an out-and-back hike to the lake, sunk in its gorge. Once at the water, follow the accessible shore for the viewpoints.

The trail is not technical, but it is long: allow the whole day from the camp, with a good packed lunch. The lake appears all at once round a scree slope, turquoise, between two vertical walls several hundred metres high.

Back along the track to At-Bashy for the Kochoy Korgon fortress and its small museum, then on to the Tash Rabat valley. The stone caravanserai is built around a central domed hall.

<Day n={7} title="Tash Rabat and the Chatyr-Kul pass" />

A long climb to the Chatyr-Kul pass at 4,025 m for the view over the lake. The track is fine up to the border post; beyond it, the last hairpins are done on foot.

<Regrets>

- Stay two nights at [Köl-Suu](#pt-kol-suu-camp) instead of one, to do the hike without the pressure of the drive back.

</Regrets>

</Region>

<Region id="issyk-koul-sud">

<Day n={8} title="Tash Rabat → Ton" />

A long driving day: At-Bashy, Naryn, Kochkor, Ottuk then Bokonbaevo, with a stop at the Manjyly-Ata springs before the night in Ton.

The south shore of Issyk-Kul is the wilder of the two. No big resorts as on the north side, but scattered villages, pebble beaches and, behind them, the snowy Terskey Ala-Too range that never leaves the horizon.

<Day n={9} title="Ton → Skazka → Karakol" />

<Tip kind="road" at="skazka">
The turn-off for Skazka canyon is about 4 km before Tosor, and the last 2 to 3 km are on a track. Stay on the visible paths: the formations erode quickly.
</Tip>

Skazka canyon, the "fairy tale", lives up to its name: ridges of red, ochre and yellow sandstone carved by erosion, explored on foot in an hour. Come in the late afternoon, when the low light sets the colours alight.

On through the Tamga Tash petroglyphs and the yurt workshop in Barskoön, then a detour into the Jeti-Ögüz valley and the Seven Bulls before Karakol.

<Day n={10} title="Karakol and Altyn Arashan" />

In Karakol, the Dungan mosque, Holy Trinity Cathedral and the history museum. In the afternoon, up through Ak-Suu to Altyn Arashan for the hot springs.

<Tip kind="road" at="altyn-arashan">
The track from Ak-Suu to Altyn Arashan is off-road all the way: allow 2 h one way for 31 km in a suitable vehicle.
</Tip>

Altyn Arashan is a small hamlet of wooden cabins at the end of a forested valley, at 2,600 m. The hot pools are simple, under a plank roof; after the track, it is the best moment of the trip.

<Regrets>

- Split the [Tash Rabat](#pt-tash-rabat) to [Ton](#pt-ton) day in two: 350 km in one day is too much.
- Keep a free half-day in [Karakol](#pt-karakol) to rest between two tracks.

</Regrets>

</Region>

<Region id="jyrgalan">

<Day n={11} title="Karakol → Jyrgalan" />

From Karakol to Jyrgalan, a former mining village, for a loop hike to Turnaluu-Köl lake. The Kök-Bel waterfall is an optional detour.

Jyrgalan turned to hiking after the mine closed. The village is quiet, surrounded by pastures, and several guesthouses hand out maps of the marked trails. The Turnaluu-Köl loop takes a day, with reasonable climbing.

<Tip kind="water" at="jyrgalan">
There is no reliable water source on the Turnaluu-Köl loop. Set off with two litres each, or a filter for the streams.
</Tip>

<Day n={12} title="North shore → Tokmok" />

The next day, the Chon Ak-Suu valley from Grigorievka, the Cholpon-Ata petroglyphs, then across through Balykchy to Konorchek canyon and its red rock formations, before the night in Tokmok.

The north shore is more built-up, more touristy, and the road is better. The Cholpon-Ata petroglyphs spread over a field of moraine boulders, with ibex and hunting scenes carved more than two thousand years ago. Konorchek, at the end of the day, means a short walk up a dry riverbed.

<Regrets>

- Spend one more night in [Jyrgalan](#pt-jyrgalan): the Turnaluu-Köl loop and then the north shore the next day is a lot.

</Regrets>

</Region>

<Region id="tchou">

<Day n={13} title="Tokmok → Kegeti" />

<Tip kind="info" at="tokmok-market">
The Tokmok livestock market runs on Sundays and starts well before sunrise. Get there early: it is all over by noon.
</Tip>

The Tokmok market is one of the liveliest moments of the trip: horses, sheep and cows change hands in the dust, among the vans and the tea sellers.

On to Kegeti for the hike to Köl-Tör lake, a steep trail up to the turquoise water, then the Kegeti waterfall.

<Tip kind="road" at="kol-tor">
Past the Kichi Kegeti valley, the road to the trailhead includes off-road sections.
</Tip>

The Köl-Tör trail climbs relentlessly for three hours through spruce forest, then comes out on a lake of an almost unreal blue. Head down before mid-afternoon: the trail is slippery in the shade.

<Day n={14} title="Chuy valley → Bishkek" />

Last driving day: Burana tower, the Suyab and Navekat ruins, the Ata-Beyit memorial and then Ala-Archa national park, before returning the car in Bishkek.

Burana tower is all that remains of Balasagun, a Silk Road trading town. A narrow inner staircase leads to the top; around it lies a field of balbals, stone steles with human faces gathered from across the valley.

<Day n={15} title="Departure" />

<Tip kind="info">
The flight home leaves very early: plan to be at Manas 2 h 30 before departure, a 35-minute drive from the centre.
</Tip>

<Regrets>

- Time Tokmok for a Sunday, for [the livestock market](#pt-tokmok-market).
- Keep a full day for [Ala-Archa](#pt-ala-archa) rather than a late afternoon.

</Regrets>

</Region>
